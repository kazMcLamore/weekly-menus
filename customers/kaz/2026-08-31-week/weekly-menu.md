It already does what Kaz asked: green beans **and** peas **and** carrots in the filling, garlic + thyme + rosemary seasoning, a thinner potato layer (2.5 lb potatoes across 12 servings vs the BBC's 900g/4), and cheddar on top. Kaz's requested changes are applied on top: **more meat (+33%), fresh green beans instead of canned, GF thickeners only, and a salt-light hand**.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 10 portions — Kaz-requested modification of the source)
**Ingredients:**
- 1.5kg ground **lamb** (true shepherd's pie) **or lean ground beef** — *whichever is available Tuesday; +33% meat vs the source per Kaz*
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 3 medium carrots, diced small
- 6 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp salt (reduced; source uses 1 tsp + seasoned broth) + ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 can (8 oz / 226g) tomato sauce (or 2½ tbsp tomato paste + ⅓ cup water)
- 1.5 tbsp **cornstarch** (GF — replaces the source's 2 tbsp all-purpose flour)
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- ½ tsp dried rosemary
- 1 cup beef broth (GF)
- 1.25 tbsp **GF Worcestershire sauce**
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 500g fresh green beans, trimmed, cut into 2–3cm pieces (replaces the source's 15oz canned French-style beans — fresher and cheaper in Playa; simmer 5–6 min in the gravy so they stay crisp-tender)
- 1.2kg russet potatoes, peeled, 1-inch chunks (**less potato than the source's 1.13kg because the batch is cut to 10 portions with more filling**)
- 4 tbsp butter + ¼ cup milk (plus more as needed)
- 120g sharp cheddar, shredded

**Method:**
1. **Potatoes:** boil the diced potato in salted water 12–15 min until fork-tender; drain well, mash with butter, milk, salt. Set aside, covered.
2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium; cook onion + carrots 6–8 min; add garlic, 30 sec.
3. Add the meat, salt, pepper; brown thoroughly, breaking it small. **Drain excess fat.**
4. Stir in tomato sauce, cornstarch, thyme, rosemary; cook 1 min. Gradually add broth + GF Worcestershire; simmer 4–5 min until the gravy thickens.
5. Stir in the green beans (simmer 5 min so they turn bright green and just tender) then the peas; off heat.
6. Spread the filling in a 9×13 dish (or 2 smaller dishes); cool 10 min; top with mash, fork-ridges, cheddar.
7. Bake **uncovered at 200°C / 400°F ~20 min**, then cheese + 5–10 min more until bubbly. Rest 10 min before portioning.

**Storage:** Fridge 4 days. Freezes beautifully (bake, cool, freeze portions up to 2 months; reheat covered ~1 hr at 175°C or microwave). Also freezes well unbaked.

#### Scaling Notes (documented v2 changes — Kaz explicitly requested)
- **Base recipe swapped from BBC no-fuss to Favorite Family Recipes' green-bean version** per Kaz's request for more vegetables, more meat, less potato, more seasoning. This is a documented, customer-requested change (not a silent swap).
- **Meat +33%** vs source (2.5 lb → 1.5kg for a 10-portion cut) — "more meat."
- **Potato reduced** to ~113g/portion (source is ~94g/portion; last week's BBC was 225g) — "less mashed potatoes," thin golden layer with the cheddar.

- **Worcestershire must be the GF bottle** already in the pantry.

### 🍖 DISH 2: Chopped Barbecue (Oven Pulled Pork, KC-Style)
**Source:** https://www.onceuponachef.com/recipes/pulled-pork-with-tangy-barbecue-sauce.html (4.88★/105 reviews, chef-tested; oven method, 195°F fork-tender)  
**Cross-check:** https://www.recipetineats.com/pulled-pork-with-bbq-sauce/ (4.97★/142) and https://www.thespruceeats.com/oven-pulled-pork-barbecue-3059506  
**Why this recipe:** Once Upon a Chef is a classically-trained chef's tested site; this pulled pork is 4.88★ from 105 reviews with 352 comments, explicitly freezer-friendly (3 months) and fridge-friendly (4 days) — ideal for the Wednesday long-braise slot. It delivers the crusty "bark" a slow cooker can't (the author confirms slow cookers won't bark). The sauce is a KC-style sweet-tangy tomato sauce enriched with the pan juices.

**Original servings:** 12 | **Scaled to:** 10 portions (1 boneless/bone-in pork butt ~2.5–2.8kg), 8 assigned + 2 buffer  

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Calories | ~400 kcal |
| Protein | 33g (33%) |
| Carbs | 22g (22%) |
| Fat | 17g (39%) |
| Fibre | 1g |

*Pork + sauce only (source: 485 kcal per 12-serving cut; our ~120–130g portion with sauce ≈ 400). Plate with borracho beans (~140) + cornbread slice (179) ≈ ~720 kcal.*

#### Macro Calculation Summary (USDA)
Pork shoulder, cooked & trimmed ≈ 250 kcal/100g → 120g ≈ 300 kcal / 27P / 17F; sauce ~3 tbsp ≈ 60–80 kcal / 13C. The source's own card (485 kcal incl. a full ⅓-cup sauce serving of 12) overstates fat because much renders off; Nagi's card for the same dish (416 kcal/36.9P/37.7C/11.3F per serving) brackets ours. Sugar is the sauce's main carb load — reduced per the salt/sweet note below.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 10 portions)
**Ingredients — Pork:**
- 1 pork butt / shoulder (bone-in preferred), **2.5–2.8kg** (buy boneless 2.2–2.5kg if that's what Chedraui has)
- 3 tsp salt (reduced ~25% from the source's 4 tsp per Kaz's "lighter hand with salt in Southern BBQ")
- 1 tbsp paprika
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- 1 tbsp garlic powder
- 1 tbsp dry mustard
- 1 tbsp (packed) dark brown sugar
- 1 tsp black pepper

**Tangy KC-Style Barbecue Sauce (GF):**
- 1 cup + 2 tbsp ketchup (GF label)
- ¾ cup cider vinegar
- ½ cup dark brown sugar (reduced from ¾ cup — Kaz: avoid overly sweet)
- ¼ cup + 2 tbsp GF mustard (spicy brown or Dijon)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- ½ tsp cayenne
- ¾ cup water (for deglazing the roasting pan)

**Method:**
1. **Tuesday night:** pat the pork dry; mix the rub; coat all over; refrigerate uncovered overnight (dry-brine = better bark + deeper seasoning).
2. Oven to **150°C / 300°F**, rack lower-middle. Roast **uncovered 6–6½ hr**, until fork-tender and **195°F / 90°C** internal (go by tenderness, not the clock).
3. While it roasts, make the sauce: ketchup + vinegar + brown sugar + mustard + garlic + cayenne in a saucepan; simmer ~10 min, stirring, until slightly thickened.
4. Rest the pork 10 min, tented. Pour off the fat; add ¾ cup water to the roasting pan over medium heat, scrape the browned bits, reduce by half; pour into the sauce and simmer 5 min.
5. **Chop/pull** the pork with two forks into chunks (discard big fat/sinew — this is "chopped" barbecue); toss with ~⅔ of the sauce, adding more to taste.

**Storage:** Fridge 4 days; **freezes 3 months** (sauce and all — reheat gently with a splash of water). Reheat microwave covered, or foil + 120°C oven ~30 min for a big batch.

#### Scaling Notes
- 1× OUC recipe (5–6 lb butt → 12 source servings) portioned into **10 Kaz-sized portions** (~120–130g + sauce each). A bone-in 2.5–2.8kg butt is the Playa-realistic size; expect a touch more buffer if you land 3kg+.
- **Salt reduced ~25%** in the rub (4 tsp → 3 tsp) per the documented Southern-week feedback; **sauce brown sugar cut from ¾ cup → ½ cup** for the same reason (Kaz: avoid overly sweet/salty).
- **GF:** ketchup (GF), GF mustard, GF Worcestershire if the sauce recipe you follow includes it (OUC's does not), cider vinegar (GF), spices — all clean. **Do not add beer** anywhere (Nagi's version uses beer in the braise — skip it; the OUC oven method needs no braising liquid).
- Sauce can be made **Tuesday evening** (keeps ≥1 week refrigerated) to unload Wednesday.

### 🫘 DISH 3: Smoky Borracho Beans (GF — no beer)
**Source:** https://www.rachelcooks.com/frijoles-borrachos/ (5★/8 votes; alcohol-free variant documented by the author)  
**Cross-check:** https://www.simplyrecipes.com/borracho-beans-recipe-8642099 (Simply Recipes — same bacon/beer/chile/spice architecture)  
**Why this recipe:** Rachel Gurk's frijoles borrachos is 5★-rated, stovetop, and — critically — includes the author's own **beer-free variant** (add 1 can fire-roasted diced tomatoes + increase molasses to 3 tbsp). Since **beer contains gluten**, that variant *is* the GF version of this dish, keeping the dark-caramel depth. Smoky, bacon-forward, chipotle-optional — the natural chopped-BBQ side. Better on day 2–3; freezes well.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes ~12 side portions; GF no-beer version)
**Ingredients:**
- 500g (2.5 cups) dried pinto beans — **soaked Tuesday night, 12+ hr**
- 7–8 slices bacon, diced
- 1¼ cups finely diced yellow onion (1 large)
- 2–3 jalapeños, ribs/seeds removed, finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 can (15 oz / 425g) **fire-roasted diced tomatoes** (replaces the beer — the source's documented no-beer variant)
- 3¾ cups low-sodium chicken broth (GF)
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- **3 tbsp molasses** (raised from 1 tbsp per the no-beer variant)
- 1¼ tsp dried Mexican oregano
- 1¼ tsp chili powder (or ½ tsp chipotle powder for smoke)
- 1 tsp salt (reduced; taste at the end)
- ¾ tsp ground cumin
- 2½ tsp fresh lime juice
- 1¼ tbsp minced fresh cilantro

**Method:**
1. Rinse + sort the beans; soak overnight (12+ hr); drain well.
2. Render the bacon in a large pot 5–7 min. Add onion + jalapeño 5 min; garlic 1 min.
3. Add the tomatoes (scrape the brown bits), beans, broth, brown sugar, molasses, oregano, chili powder, salt, cumin. Boil, then partially cover and **simmer 2½ hr**, stirring occasionally, until tender and saucy (top up with broth if it dries).
4. Off heat: lime juice + cilantro. Taste for salt.

**Storage:** Fridge 4 days; freezer 3 months; better on day 2. Reheat microwave/stovetop.

#### Scaling Notes

- Optional smoky boost: ½ tsp smoked paprika or chipotle powder in place of the chilli powder.

### 🥬 DISH 4: One-Pot Braised Southern Cabbage with Bacon
**Source:** https://www.thekitchn.com/recipe-southern-cabbage-with-bacon-240518 (Chadwick Boyd — Southern Food Writing Conference organizer; naturally GF)  
**Cross-check:** https://bellyfull.net/fried-cabbage/ and https://www.deepsouthdish.com/2011/12/southern-fried-cabbage.html (same bacon-fat + cider-vinegar architecture)  
**Why this recipe:** The Kitchn's Southern braised cabbage is one pot, ~45 min, gluten-free, cheap, and holds 4 days in the fridge. USDA check: cabbage ~120g ≈ 28 kcal; bacon ~15g ≈ 60 kcal/5F; vinegar/broth ≈ 10 kcal → ~100–110 kcal per small side; we use the source's 171 as the upper bound and pack to ~90–110 kcal.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes ~12 portions, 2.5×)
**Ingredients:**
- 12–13 slices thick-cut bacon, cut into 1cm pieces
- Olive oil as needed
- 12 cloves garlic, finely chopped
- 2 medium green cabbages (~4–4.5 lb / ~2kg total), cut through the core into 1cm-thick wedges
- 2½ cups low-sodium chicken broth (GF)
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tbsp kosher salt (reduced; taste at the end)
- 4 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- Black pepper

**Method:**
1. In a large Dutch oven over medium-high, crisp the bacon 8–10 min; remove to a paper towel. Ensure ~3 tbsp fat remains (top with olive oil if short).
2. Add garlic; stir. Add cabbage wedges cut-side down (they'll stack — that's fine); brown 4–5 min undisturbed, then rotate top-to-bottom and cook 7–8 min more until wilted and browned at the edges.
3. Lower to medium; add broth, bay leaf, salt. Simmer, stirring every few minutes, until tender and the liquid is nearly gone, **20–25 min** (scaled batch: add 5–10 min and stir more often).
4. Off heat: stir in the vinegar; season; scatter the bacon on top.

**Storage:** Fridge 4 days (holds well — this is the fish plate's green). Freezes acceptably (softer on thaw). Reheat microwave.

#### Scaling Notes
- Scaled 2.5× (2 heads) because cabbage cooks down hard — directly per Kaz's "make green batches substantially larger" instruction.
- Cabbage wedges stay core-attached (the recipe's signature "fans"); a 5-quart+ pot is needed — work in two batches if crowded.
- Bay leaf scales 1→2–3; **salt reduced ~30%** vs the source's 1½ tsp/4–6 servings because the bacon carries salt.

**Ingredients:**
- ½ cup unsalted butter, melted
- ⅔ cup granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 cup buttermilk (or milk + 1 tbsp vinegar)
- 2 cups yellow cornmeal
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 can (15 oz) **GF-labelled cream-style corn**
- 1 can (15 oz) **GF-labelled sweet corn**, drained

**Method:**
1. Grease a cast-iron skillet or 8×8 dish. Oven **190°C / 375°F**.
2. Whisk butter, sugar, eggs, buttermilk; fold in cornmeal, baking soda, salt, creamed corn, drained corn.
3. Bake ~40 min until set. Cool; cut into 16.

**Storage:** Room temp 1–2 days; fridge 5–7; freeze 2–3 months. Reheat microwave/oven.

#### Scaling Notes
Exactly the approved recipe at 1× (the 08-10 batch was 2×). GF note: check the creamed-corn and corn labels.

### 🍚 ARROZ ROJO — repeat recipe, carb (fish plates)
**Source:** https://www.rickbayless.com/recipe/red-tomato-rice/ (approved 2026-06-29 + 2026-08-24; cross-check Mely Martínez)

**Original yield:** ~6 cups | **Scaled to:** 1× (unchanged), ~15 ⅓-cup portions (12 assigned + buffer)

Per portion (⅓ cup / 80g): **115 kcal / 2P / 22C / 1F.**

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (1× the approved recipe)
- 1 can (15 oz) diced fire-roasted tomatoes, drained (half of a second can also works)
- ½ medium white onion + 2 garlic cloves
- 1½ tbsp vegetable oil
- 1½ cups white rice
- 1¾ cups chicken broth (GF)
- 3 serrano chiles, slit
- ¼ cup parsley
- ¾ tsp salt (salted broth)

**Method (unchanged):** blend tomato+onion+garlic → fry rice in oil 5–6 min → add puree 2–3 min → add broth, boil, add chiles/parsley/salt → lowest heat, covered, **20 min, do not stir** → fluff, rest 5 min. ~6 cups.

**Storage:** Fridge 4–5 days; short Mon–Tue freeze acceptable (2–4 days). Reheat with a sprinkle of water.

#### Scaling Notes
Unchanged from the approved formula; only the portion target changed (fish plates only: ~12 portions needed).

### ⚠️ NOTES & CONSIDERATIONS

- **Two carbs this week (approved by Kaz):** arroz rojo with the fish, cornbread with the BBQ, and the pie stands alone. Cornbread is the previously approved Show Me the Yummy recipe, reused exactly.
- **Beer = gluten.** The borracho beans are made **without beer** using the source's documented no-beer variant (fire-roasted tomatoes + 3 tbsp molasses). Do not let the assistant add a Negra Modelo "for authenticity."
- **Salt-light rub:** the pork rub is cut ~25% vs the source per the documented Southern-week feedback. Taste before adding more.
- **Kaz's feedback encoded:** no overly sweet/salty beans (molasses kept at the no-beer floor, brown sugar trimmed); homemade BBQ sauce (GF, salt-light).
- **Fish is the weakest freezer** (same caveat as 08-24): eat fish Fri/Sat first; if Kaz prefers, swap a Mon–Tue fish plate for extra pie/BBQ.
- **Wednesday is a long oven day:** cornbread (40 min) → pork (6–6½ hr uncovered at 150°C) → pie (20–25 min at 200°C after the pork exits). If the cook starts by 6:30–7:00 AM, everything lands before dinner service. **Wednesday lunch = prior-batch leftovers** (documented exception for this week); if the pork starts by 6 AM, Wednesday lunch can be fresh BBQ + pie instead.
- **The pork butt is big (2.5–2.8kg).** Nagi's note: don't go much under ~2kg or it cooks too fast and lacks flavour; leftovers freeze "100% perfectly," so a bigger butt is fine (extra buffer).
- **Cornbread is frozen for Mon–Tue** (approved behaviour from the 08-10 week); beans and cabbage freeze well; fish is the weakest freezer (4 portions Mon–Tue, eat fish Fri/Sat first).
- **Leftover old-batch portions** (old pie, butter chicken, dal, green beans, rice) should be eaten Wednesday as bonus lunches / buffer — don't let them linger past ~Wednesday.
- **Ketchup, mustard, Worcestershire, bouillon, creamed corn: check GF labels** — this week's GF watch-points.
