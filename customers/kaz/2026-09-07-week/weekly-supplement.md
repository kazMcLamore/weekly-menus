# PART 1 — Shopping Guide

## Ingredients that may be unfamiliar

### Dried chiles: guajillo, ancho, chile de árbol
<img src="https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/I/81lTHcBLgOL.jpg" align="right" width="100" alt="Dried chiles">
**What they are:** dried chile pods — the backbone of the red chile sauce. Look for them loose in the produce aisle or bagged in the spice section: **guajillo** (long, smooth, brick-red, mild-to-medium, tangy-sweet), **ancho** (dried poblano, wide and heart-shaped, dark mahogany, sweet-smoky), **chile de árbol** (small, slim, bright red, very hot — the heat). This week uses all three.  
**Taste:** guajillo = balanced, slightly fruity; ancho = rich, raisiny; árbol = serious heat.  
**Where to buy:** 🟢 any Playa supermarket (Chedraui/Walmart/Soriana — "pasillo de chiles secos") or the local mercado/DAC — cheapest there.  
**Substitutions:** if only ground chile powder is available, the author gives measured equivalents: 3 tbsp ground guajillo + 1 tbsp ground ancho + ¼ tsp ground árbol per full batch (start small — árbol is hot).  
**Handling:** don't rinse dried chiles (it washes off their flavour oils) — wipe with a damp paper towel; discard the rehydrating water (it can be dusty).  
<br clear="right">

### Tomatillos (tomates verdes)
<img src="https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0603/4892/4151/files/BONNIE-PLANTS_tomatillo-iStock-946666554-2400px.jpg" align="right" width="100" alt="Tomatillos">
**What it is:** small green fruits in a papery lantern husk (look like little lanterns). They are NOT unripe tomatoes — they're tart, bright, and the base of salsa verde. In Playa they're everywhere and cheap; just peel the husk and rinse the sticky film off.  
**Taste:** bright, tangy, slightly herbal. Broiling them chars the skin and deepens the flavour.  
<br clear="right">

### Pimienta gorda / allspice
**What it is:** round, dark-brown dried berry that looks like a large peppercorn; smells like a mix of clove + cinnamon + pepper. Used in tiny amounts (½ tsp) in the lentils — it's the classic Mexican "pimienta gorda." 🟢 any supermarket spice aisle.  
**Substitution:** a small pinch of ground cloves + cinnamon if unavailable (DAC carries it).

### Plantain + pineapple in the lentils (the Oaxaca twist)
**What it is:** the lentejas recipe comes from a chef in Oaxaca; the unusual part is sweet **plantain slices and pineapple chunks** cooked into the stew — sweet-savoury contrast with the bacon and lentils. It sounds strange but it's a beloved Oaxacan combination.  
**Where to buy:** 🟢 plantains and pineapple are cheap and everywhere in Playa (choose a plantain that is mostly yellow with black patches = ripe-sweet).  
**Substitution:** no plantain? use sweet plantain slices or extra potato; no fresh pineapple? a few chunks of canned pineapple (drained) work.

### Chorizo & shrimp
**Chorizo (papas con chorizo):** 🟢 any supermarket (Cacique/El Mexicano brands or the butcher's fresh chorizo). It releases orange-red grease when cooked — **drain the excess grease** after frying (the recipe author confirms store-bought chorizo is greasy; drain on paper towels).  
**Shrimp:** buy large, already peeled and deveined. Frozen is fine — thaw overnight in the fridge, pat dry. Camarones cocidos never freeze.

# PART 2 — Cooking Tips (cited techniques)

### 1. Soak dried chiles gently — never boil them hard
Isabel Orozco-Moore (Isabel Eats): rehydrate the stemmed, seeded dried chiles by bringing the water to a boil, then **remove the pot from the heat and let them steep 20 minutes** — boiling chiles hard gives the sauce a bitter taste. Blend with 1 cup of the chile-soaking water, oregano, salt, garlic and cumin until smooth. The sauce also freezes on its own for up to 6 months — make it ahead. (Source: https://www.isabeleats.com/chile-colorado-recipe/)

### 2. Sear in batches, then braise low
Isabel again: toss the beef with the thickener + salt + pepper, and sear in **two batches** so the meat browns instead of steaming. Return all the beef, pour in the red sauce + broth + bay leaves, cover, simmer 45 minutes, then uncover and reduce 15 more minutes so the sauce thickens. Low and slow is the whole technique. (Source: https://www.isabeleats.com/chile-colorado-recipe/)

### 3. Broil + steam + peel the verde peppers
Isabel (Chicken Chile Verde): broil the tomatillos and peppers until blackened and blistered, then cover 5 minutes to **steam and loosen the skins** — pull the loose skin off before blending. The char is where the smoky verde flavour comes from. (Source: https://www.isabeleats.com/chicken-chile-verde/)

### 4. Shrimp: pink, not grey — 2 minutes, never more
Maggie Unzueta (In Mama Maggie's Kitchen): add the shrimp to the hot pan with the onion, **stir constantly 2 minutes until just pink, then add the sauce on LOW heat 2–3 minutes**. Overcooked shrimp turns rubbery — watch the colour, not the clock. Her other tips: buy peeled/deveined shrimp, thaw frozen shrimp overnight in the fridge and pat dry, use the **diabla sauce ahead-of-time** (it keeps 5 days in the fridge or 6 months frozen — make it Wednesday). (Source: https://inmamamaggieskitchen.com/camarones-a-la-diabla-mexican-deviled-shrimp/)

### 5. Fry the potatoes before the chorizo; drain the grease
Ana Frias (Muy Delish): fry the cubed potatoes **first** in a single layer (~10 min, stirring) so they crisp, then cook the chorizo, then fold the potatoes back in. The author and readers both flag that store-bought chorizo releases a lot of grease — **drain the excess before returning the potatoes**, otherwise the dish swims in red oil. (Source: https://www.muydelish.com/chorizo-papas/)

### 6. The Oaxacan lentil trick: sweet + smoky
Emily, from Casa Crespo (Oaxaca — Food52 Test Kitchen-Approved): the signature of this lentejas version is cooking **plantain + pineapple** into the lentils alongside bacon — sweet against smoky-savoury. Simmer the lentils separately first so they stay intact, then finish them in the fruit-tomato base. And save your **cilantro stems** — they enrich the broth (freeze extras for future broths). (Source: https://food52.com/recipes/38323-lentejas-mexican-lentil-stew)
