# PART 1 — SHOPPING GUIDE

## New or "unusual" ingredients this week

### Barilla Gluten-Free pasta — spaghetti AND penne (two different boxes)
<img src="https://www.grocery.com/store/image/cache/catalog/barilla/barilla-gluten-free-pasta-spaghetti-12-ounce-pack--1-500x500.jpg" align="right" width="100" alt="GF pasta">

**What it is:** pasta made from corn + rice (no wheat), certified gluten-free. **This week we use TWO shapes:** spaghetti (the long strands) for the meatballs and shrimp, and **penne** (the short ribbed tubes) for the creamy chicken — short ridged shapes grip cream sauces best.
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart Playa (pasta aisle — blue Barilla box that clearly says "GLUTEN FREE") or Chedraui Selecto. **Verify the shelf on Tuesday** — if only one shape is available, use it for everything; if none → baked polenta (cornmeal already in the pantry).
**Cooking difference:** cooks 1–2 minutes faster than wheat and stays firmer for reheats if pulled at al dente. Always reserve the pasta water.<br clear="right">

### Sun-dried tomatoes in oil
**What they are:** tomatoes dried in the sun and sold in jars, submerged in herbed oil. They look like thin, dark-red wrinkled strips. Intensely sweet-concentrated tomato flavour. They are THE signature ingredient of the chicken's creamy sauce.
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart (import aisle, or with the pickles/jarred vegetables — look for red glass jars) or Chedraui Selecto.
**Substitutes (in order):** (1) omit and make the sauce with tomato paste + cream cheese only (still good, less Italian); (2) roast cherry tomatoes in the oven until wrinkled, with a drizzle of oil.<br clear="right">

### Gluten-free Italian-style breadcrumbs
**What it is:** dried ground bread that binds the meatballs. Regular breadcrumbs contain wheat — **this one must say "GLUTEN FREE" / "SIN GLUTEN" on the label**. Looks like regular breadcrumbs (toasted ground bread, sometimes pre-seasoned with Italian herbs).
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart or Chedraui Selecto (bread/oat aisle or imports).
**Substitutes (in order):** (1) buy a GF loaf (baguette or boxed bread), dry it in a 150°C oven for 15 min until toasted, and blend it with ½ tsp oregano + ¼ tsp garlic powder — homemade GF breadcrumbs; (2) no breadcrumbs at all: use 3 extra Tbsp grated parmesan + 2 Tbsp GF flour in the mix (slightly denser meatballs, but they work).<br clear="right">

### GF 1-to-1 flour (gluten-free flour mix)
**What it is:** a blend of gluten-free flours (rice + corn + starches) that replaces wheat flour MEASURE FOR MEASURE in savoury recipes. We use it ONLY to dredge the chicken in the cream dish (it builds a crust and thickens the sauce).
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart/Chedraui Selecto (flour aisle or health section; brands that show up: Bob's Red Mill, King Arthur).
**Substitutes (in order):** (1) cornstarch (maicena) — dredge with HALF the amount (50g) and shake off the excess well; (2) plain rice flour; (3) skip the dredge — less browning but the sauce still works.<br clear="right">

### Shallots
**What they are:** small elongated onions, purple-pink on the outside — like a purple onion but smaller and sweeter. Peel like garlic: separated layers. Softer, sweeter flavour than onion.
**Where to buy:** 🟢 Walmart/Chedraui (produce, next to the small onions).
**Substitute:** one small white onion, minced very fine — works the same.<br clear="right">

### Media crema (half-and-half)
**What it is:** the tetra-pack media crema (Nestlé or similar) you already know — it's the Mexican version of American "half-and-half" (half cream, half milk). Goes into the chicken's cream sauce along with light cream cheese.
**Where to buy:** 🟢 any supermarket, dairy aisle.
**Substitute:** crema ácida thinned with a little milk, or just more broth + cream cheese (less creamy but works).<br clear="right">

### Capers + balsamic (last week's recap)
**What they are:** briny pickled flower buds (capers) and the sweet Italian vinegar (balsamic) — both finish the peperonata. We bought them last week — **check the pantry before buying**.
**Where to buy:** 🟢 any supermarket.<br clear="right">

### Dry white wine (optional)
**For what:** the shrimp scampi. A cheap sauvignon blanc is perfect. **You can skip it:** swap in GF chicken broth + an extra squeeze of lemon — the source explicitly approves this.
**Where to buy:** 🟢 Walmart/Soriana (wine section). Don't buy "cooking wine" — it tastes like vinegar.<br clear="right">

# PART 2 — COOKING TIPS

### 1. Meatballs: brown them in the OVEN, on an ungreased sheet
Jenn Segal (Once Upon a Chef, classically trained chef): bake the meatballs on an **ungreased, unlined sheet** — direct metal contact browns them and makes turning easier. At 10 minutes turn them with a metal spatula (they stick a bit — scrape under firmly). Ten minutes more and they go **straight into simmering marinara for 10 minutes**, covered loosely. From the parmesan label: look for imported "Parmigiano-Reggiano" (the rind carries the embossed name) — generic parmesan tastes flat. (Source: https://www.onceuponachef.com/recipes/spaghetti-and-meatballs.html)

### 2. Creamy chicken: pound it, dredge it thin, and the flavour secret
Gina Homolka (Skinnytaste): pound the chicken breasts to even thickness BEFORE cooking — they cook evenly and the thin ends don't dry out. The flour dredge (Italian piccata technique) builds crust and thickens the sauce — GF 1-to-1 flour works the same, just shake off the excess well. Use the oil from the sun-dried tomato jar to sear — it carries tomato flavour. And low heat when the cream cheese goes in: a hard boil breaks the sauce. (Source: https://www.skinnytaste.com/marry-me-chicken/)

### 3. Shrimp: dry, not crowded, "C" shape
Natasha Kravchuk (Natasha's Kitchen, NYT bestselling author): pat the shrimp DRY with paper towels FIRST — wet shrimp steams instead of searing. Big pan, ONE layer — crowded shrimp steam and turn rubbery. Cook 1–2 min per side until pink and curled into a "C" — an "O" means overdone. Use a non-reactive pan (no bare cast iron or aluminium) because the wine and lemon react with the metal. (Source: https://natashaskitchen.com/shrimp-scampi-recipe/)

### 4. Green beans: long, covered braise with patience
Kate Dowse (Gluten Free Alchemist, Italian family recipe): after softening the onion and garlic in butter + oil, add the tomatoes, bring to a boil, add the beans and **cover** — gentle simmer 45 minutes, stirring occasionally. Older beans get de-stringed (pull the seam off the spine). Don't pull them out crisp — this dish IS soft, almost confit in tomato. (Source: https://www.glutenfreealchemist.com/green-beans-cooked-in-tomatoes/)

### 5. Peperonata: start in a COLD pan
Domenica Marchetti (The Mediterranean Dish — author of 8 Italian cookbooks): put the oil + peppers + onions + garlic in a **cold skillet**, then turn the heat to medium — the slow warm-up coaxes out the peppers' sweetness without browning them bitter. Stew gently ~30 minutes total, finish with balsamic off the boil, and rest 10 minutes. Keeps 5 days in the fridge and **improves as it sits**. (Source: https://www.themediterraneandish.com/peperonata-recipe/)

### 6. GF pasta: pasta water = the sauce's glue
Nagi Maehashi (RecipeTin Eats): before draining, stir the pasta hard and scoop out a cup of the cloudy water — **the starch in the water emulsifies with the sauce's fat and makes it cling to the pasta** instead of pooling at the bottom of the container. When reheating, a splash of that water (or plain water) revives the sauce. And for creamy sauces: microwave reheat in short bursts, stirring halfway. (Source: https://www.recipetineats.com/vodka-pasta/ — pasta water technique section)
