# PART 1 — Shopping Guide

## Ingredients that may be unfamiliar

### Toor dal (split pigeon peas / lentejas amarillas partidas)
<img src="https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/41t8Be5BiOL.jpg" align="right" width="100" alt="Toor dal">
**What it is:** small yellow split lentils (split pigeon peas). They look like small yellow lentils — flat, light yellow, no skin. This is the base of the dal tadka.  
**Taste/texture:** cooks down soft and creamy, mild earthy flavour; it absorbs the spices and tadka well.  
**Where to buy:** 🔴 **DAC Market** (30 Av. entre Calle 14 y 14 Bis) — best for Indian lentils; or **Amazon Mexico** (amazon.com.mx, search "toor dal"). Walmart/Chedraui international aisle sometimes stock it.  
**Substitutions (priority order):** (1) yellow split peas (lentejas amarillas partidas) — closest, found in big supermarkets; (2) masoor dal (red split lentils) — cook faster (~20 min), slightly different colour/texture but fine; (3) moong dal (split yellow mung beans).  
**GF note:** lentils are naturally GF, but buy sealed packs (bulk-bin lentils can be cross-contaminated with grain dust).  
<br clear="right">

### Kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves / hojas de fenogreco secas)
<img src="https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/I/71TWPycHNvL.jpg" align="right" width="100" alt="Kasuri methi">
**What it is:** dried leaves of the fenugreek plant. Small, crumbly, olive-green dried leaves with a strong, savoury, slightly bitter/maple-like aroma. Crushed between the palms and stirred into the dal at the end — it is what gives restaurant dal its distinctive fragrance.  
**Where to buy:** 🔴 **DAC Market** or **Amazon Mexico** (search "kasuri methi"). A small pack lasts months.  
**Substitutions:** there is no true substitute. If you truly cannot find it: add an extra pinch of cumin + a few chopped fresh coriander/mint leaves at the end. The dal is still good without it, just less aromatic.  
<br clear="right">

### Garam masala
<img src="https://hebbarskitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/garam-masala-recipe-how-to-make-homemade-garam-masala-spice-mix-powder-1-1024x682.jpeg" align="right" width="100" alt="Garam masala">
**What it is:** a warm Indian spice blend (the name means "warming spices"). Brown powder, fragrant — typically coriander, cumin, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, black pepper, sometimes dried chilli. Used in both the butter chicken and the dal.  
**Where to buy:** 🟡 **Walmart / Chedraui international aisle** (common brand: McCormick or local), or DAC Market.  
**Substitutions:** buy a ready-made jar (easiest). Or make your own — see DIY below. Do not substitute plain curry powder (different flavour).  
<br clear="right">

### Ghee (clarified butter)
**What it is:** butter with the milk solids and water removed — pure golden fat used in Indian cooking. Higher smoke point than butter, nutty flavour. Used to cook the butter chicken and for the dal tadka.  
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart/Chedraui international aisle (brand e.g. Nanak or local), or DAC.  
**Substitutions (priority):** (1) make your own — melt butter in a pan over low heat ~15 min, skim the white foam off the top, pour the clear golden fat off the milk solids at the bottom (that clear fat is ghee); (2) use regular butter (it will foam more and brown faster — watch the heat); (3) neutral oil for the cooking fat (loses some flavour).

### Passata (tomato passata / jitomate licuado colado)
**What it is:** smooth, strained raw tomato puree (just tomatoes, no additives). Used for the butter chicken sauce.  
**Where to buy:** 🟢 Walmart/Chedraui (in the pasta-sauce aisle, often labelled "passata" or "puré de jitomate").  
**Substitutions:** blend a can of whole/diced tomatoes until completely smooth and pass it through a sieve — that is passata. (Do not use ketchup or "tomato sauce" with added sugar/herbs.)

### GF Worcestershire sauce (salsa inglesa GF)
**What it is:** the dark, tangy, savoury sauce used in the shepherd's pie filling. Standard Worcestershire (Lea & Perrins) contains **barley malt → NOT gluten-free**.  
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart/Chedraui international aisle (look for a GF-labelled brand such as The Wizard's, or Heinz GF), or DAC, or Amazon Mexico.  
**Substitutions (priority):** (1) GF Worcestershire; (2) mix 2 tsp tamari (GF soy sauce) + 1 tsp tamarind paste or lemon juice + ½ tsp molasses/brown sugar — approximates the tangy-savoury effect; (3) omit and add an extra splash of beef stock + a pinch of brown sugar.  
**GF note:** this is a key watch-point — do not use regular Worcestershire.

### Huachinango (red snapper / pargo rojo)
**What it is:** the traditional fish for pescado a la veracruzana. A firm white ocean fish.  
**Where to buy:** 🟢 any fish counter in Playa (Chedraui, Mega Soriana, Walmart, local pescaderías / mercados). Ask for "huachinango" or "pargo rojo" fillets (~150 g each).  
**Substitutions:** any firm white fish fillet — tilapia, mojarra, basa, or merluza. The recipe is built for this.

## DIY garam masala (if you can't find it)
Toast in a dry pan until fragrant, then grind: 2 tbsp coriander seeds, 1 tbsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp black peppercorns, 1 tsp green cardamom pods (seeds), 1 small cinnamon stick (~2 cm), 4 cloves, 1 dried bay leaf. Grind to a fine powder. Store in a jar. (Source: common Indian-home blend; corroborated by Hebbars Kitchen, https://hebbarskitchen.com/homemade-garam-masala-recipe/.)

## GF watch-points for this week
1. **Worcestershire sauce** — must be GF (regular has barley malt).
2. **Beef and chicken bouillon/stock** — check the label; some bouillon cubes contain gluten. Use a GF-labelled brand.
3. **Asafoetida (hing)** in the dal — common hing is cut with wheat flour. **Skip it** unless you have a GF-certified brand. The dal is excellent without it.
4. **Shepherd's pie uses no flour** — the gravy thickens by reduction, so no thickener swap is needed. If you want a thicker gravy, use **cornstarch** (maicena) slurry, never wheat flour.
5. **Lentils/spices from bulk bins** — prefer sealed packs to avoid grain-dust cross-contamination.

# PART 2 — Cooking Tips (cited techniques)

These techniques are the ones the assistant will actually use this week. Each is cited to a verified online source.

### 1. Yogurt marinade + don't wipe the chicken (Butter Chicken)
Nagi Maehashi (RecipeTin Eats): marinate the chicken in yogurt + spices for at least 3 hours (overnight is best) — the yogurt tenderises the meat and the spices infuse it. When cooking, **take the chicken out of the marinade but do not shake or wipe it off** — the clinging marinade carries flavour into the pan. Cook in a hot pan in **batches so the chicken sears instead of stewing** in its own juice. Then build the sauce in the same pan. (Source: https://www.recipetineats.com/butter-chicken/)

### 2. Tadka — blooming spices in hot ghee (Dal Tadka)
Swasthi Shreekanth (Swasthi's Recipes): *tadka* (also called *chaunk*) is the technique of frying whole spices in hot ghee/oil to release their essential oils, then pouring that infused fat over the cooked dal. Heat the ghee, add cumin seeds and let them **crackle and splutter** before adding the garlic and dried chilies. **Do not brown the garlic** — it turns bitter; fry only until aromatic, then stir in the ground chilli and pour immediately over the dal. (Source: https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/dal-tadka/)

### 3. Toast the rice before the liquid (Arroz Rojo)
Rick Bayless (and Mely Martínez, Mexico in My Kitchen): the secret to fluffy, separate arroz rojo grains is to **fry the raw rice in oil for 5–6 minutes before adding the tomato puree and broth** — the oil coats each grain and prevents them from clumping. Once the broth goes in, **do not stir and do not lift the lid** during the 20-minute simmer; stirring releases starch and makes the rice gummy. Fluff gently with a fork only after cooking. (Sources: https://www.rickbayless.com/recipe/red-tomato-rice/ ; https://www.mexicoinmykitchen.com/mexican-rice-recipe-authentic/)

### 4. Build the Veracruz sauce first, then add the fish
Douglas Cullen (Mexican Food Journal): cook the tomato-onion-garlic sauce **for 15 minutes until the tomatoes release their liquid**, then crush the vegetables with a masher, add the broth/olives/capers and **simmer 15 more minutes** to develop the flavour — *then* sear the fish briefly in a separate pan and nestle it into the sauce for the last 10 minutes. Adding the fish too early overcooks it and stops the sauce from developing depth. (Source: https://mexicanfoodjournal.com/veracruz-style-white-fish/)

### 5. Brown the mince and dry the potatoes (Shepherd's Pie)
BBC Good Food: brown the lamb/beef thoroughly over high heat — this is where the flavour comes from (the Maillard reaction); spoon off excess fat but keep a little. Cook the tomato purée for a couple of minutes to caramelise it. For fluffy mash, **drain the boiled potatoes and let them steam-dry in the colander a few minutes** before mashing, and use a floury potato variety. After baking, **let the pie rest 10 minutes** so it sets and portions neatly. (Source: https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/no-fuss-shepherds-pie)

### 6. Blanch then sauté the beans, and mince garlic with a knife (Green Beans)
Nagi Maehashi (RecipeTin Eats): boil the green beans **4 minutes** until bright green and barely tender, drain and cool — this sets the colour and texture. Then sauté with garlic just 2 minutes. **Mince the garlic with a knife, not a garlic press** — a press makes the garlic wet and pasty so it spits and burns instead of turning into crispy golden bits. You can blanch the beans ahead, refrigerate, and finish the sauté in 2 minutes on demand. (Source: https://www.recipetineats.com/garlic-sauteed-green-beans/)
