# PART 1 — Shopping Guide

## Ingredients that may be unfamiliar

### Molasses (melaza)
**What it is:** the thick, dark, bittersweet syrup left over from refining cane sugar. Looks like a very dark, slow-pouring syrup (thicker than honey, almost black). It gives the BBQ sauce and the borracho beans their deep caramel flavour.  
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart / Chedraui (baking aisle, often labelled "melaza" or "miel de caña").  
**Substitutions (priority):** (1) piloncillo dissolved in a little hot water (1 cone ≈ ¼ cup) — same deep cane flavour, sold everywhere in Playa; (2) dark corn syrup + a spoon of brown sugar; (3) honey (sweeter, lighter — reduce the added brown sugar slightly).  
**GF note:** molasses is naturally GF — check the label anyway.

### Apple cider vinegar (vinagre de sidra de manzana)
**What it is:** tangy, fruity vinegar made from apples. Used in the BBQ sauce, the cabbage, and (in tiny amounts) nothing else this week.  
**Where to buy:** 🟢 any supermarket (Walmart/Chedraui/Soriana).  
**GF note:** apple cider vinegar IS gluten-free. **Avoid "vinagre de malta" (malt vinegar) — that one contains barley.** Any plain white or apple vinegar is safe; malt is the only dangerous one.

### Smoked paprika (pimentón / paprika ahumado)
**What it is:** paprika dried over oak smoke — it is what makes oven barbecue taste "smoky" without a smoker. 🟡 Walmart/Chedraui international or spice aisle ("pimentón ahumado").  
**Substitution:** regular paprika + ¼ tsp chipotle powder, or 2–3 drops of liquid smoke in the sauce.

### Dry mustard / mustard powder (mostaza en polvo)
**What it is:** ground mustard seeds — a yellow powder, sharp and pungent. Used in the pork rub and the BBQ sauce.  
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart/Chedraui spice section ("mostaza en polvo").  
**Substitutions:** use an equal amount of **prepared yellow/Dijon mustard in the sauce** (swap 1 tbsp powder ≈ 1 tbsp prepared mustard, added at the end), and for the rub simply omit it (or add ¼ tsp extra garlic powder). Prepared mustard is the easier find — always check the label says GF.

### Fire-roasted canned tomatoes (jitomate asado en lata)
**What it is:** diced tomatoes that were fire-roasted before canning — smoky-sweet. Used in the borracho beans (the GF stand-in for beer) and optionally in the arroz rojo.  
**Where to buy:** 🟡 Walmart/Chedraui (canned tomato aisle; brands: Herdez, La Costeña).  
**Substitutions:** regular canned diced tomatoes + ½ tsp smoked paprika or a chopped chipotle in adobo — you lose a little smoke, the beans still work.

### Chipotle in adobo / chipotle powder
**What it is:** smoked jalapeño (chipotle) — canned in adobo sauce or ground. Smoky heat, the backbone of borracho beans' smokiness. 🟢 any Playa supermarket (Latin aisle).

### Molasses check + GF notes
- **NO BEER anywhere.** Traditional borracho beans are simmered in beer — gluten. We use the source's documented no-beer version (broth + fire-roasted tomatoes + 3 tbsp molasses). Never "just a little."
- Ketchup, mustard, Worcestershire: buy **GF-labelled** versions (the Worcestershire bottle from last week is GF — keep using it).
- Bouillon (beef + chicken): check the label; use a GF-labelled brand.
- Cornbread cans: check the cream-style corn and corn cans for GF labels (most are, but the canning starch varies).
- Bacon: plain bacon is GF; avoid flavoured/honey-cured bacons with additive glazes.

# PART 2 — Cooking Tips (cited techniques)

### 1. Cook the pork to temperature, not the clock — then rest it
Jennifer Segal (Once Upon a Chef): roast the rubbed pork butt **uncovered at 150°C (300°F) for 6–6½ hours until fork-tender and 90°C (195°F) internal** — the long low roast builds the dark, crusty "bark" that makes chopped barbecue taste like pit BBQ. Tent with foil and rest ~10 minutes so the juices redistribute. Use a thermometer in the thickest part; a 3-lb butt takes ~4–5 hours, a 5–6-lb butt the full 6–6½. (Source: https://www.onceuponachef.com/recipes/pulled-pork-with-tangy-barbecue-sauce.html)

### 2. Shred gently — never baby food
Nagi Maehashi (RecipeTin Eats): pull the pork apart with **two forks, gently** — it should be tender but still have the texture of meat. Never use a food processor or stir it like soup; toss gently with sauce so you keep meaty shreds. The author also confirms the pork **freezes perfectly even after saucing** — ideal for Monday–Tuesday portions. (Source: https://www.recipetineats.com/pulled-pork-with-bbq-sauce/)

### 3. Build the sauce with the pan juices (the "fond")
Jenn Segal again: after roasting, pour off the fat, add ¾ cup water to the hot roasting pan, and **scrape up every browned bit** — that fond is pure flavour. Reduce it by half, then stir it into the simmering BBQ sauce for 5 minutes. This is what makes a homemade sauce taste "restaurant" instead of "bottled." (Source: https://www.onceuponachef.com/recipes/pulled-pork-with-tangy-barbecue-sauce.html)

### 4. Soak the beans (it matters for more than time)
Rachel Gurk (Rachel Cooks): soak dried pinto beans at least 12 hours, drain and rinse well — soaking shortens cooking **and removes much of the compounds that cause gassiness** (Bean Institute). Simmer partially covered ~2½ hours, stirring occasionally, adding broth if they dry out; stir in the lime juice and cilantro only at the end. Beans improve overnight — they're better on day 2. (Source: https://www.rachelcooks.com/frijoles-borrachos/)

### 5. Cabbage "fans," not shreds
Chadwick Boyd (The Kitchn): cut the cabbage **through the core into wedges and keep them attached at the core** — the wedges hold together as soft "fans" instead of turning to mush. Brown them cut-side down in bacon fat without stirring, then braise in broth until the liquid is gone, and add the **cider vinegar off the heat** so the tang stays bright. (Source: https://www.thekitchn.com/recipe-southern-cabbage-with-bacon-240518)

### 6. Drain the filling, cool it, then layer the mash
Erica Walker (Favorite Family Recipes): brown the meat well and **drain the fat** — a greasy filling makes the mash slide. Let the filling cool a few minutes before spreading the mash so the layers stay clean. The author's signature: **French-style green beans + peas** stirred in at the end, thyme + rosemary, and a cheddar blanket on top. Let the baked pie rest 10 minutes before cutting so portions hold. (Source: https://www.favfamilyrecipes.com/easy-shepherds-pie/)
