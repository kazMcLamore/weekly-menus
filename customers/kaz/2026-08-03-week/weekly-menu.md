### 🍗 DISH 1: Lower-Salt Chopped Carolina Pork
**Source:** https://www.tasteofhome.com/recipes/carolina-style-pork-barbecue/  
**Cross-check:** https://heygrillhey.com/carolina-style-pulled-pork/  
**Why this recipe:** Taste of Home’s Test Kitchen recipe has 27 reviews and an oven/Dutch-oven path; Hey Grill Hey (4.97/5 from 32 votes) confirms the shoulder, low-and-slow, vinegar-forward Carolina approach. It was Kaz’s favourite last time, now deliberately lowered in salt.
**Original servings:** 14 | **Scaled to:** 8 portions

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | 335 kcal |
| Protein | 35g (42%) |
| Carbs | 6g (7%) |
| Fat | 19g (51%) |
| Fibre | 1g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
USDA FoodData Central inputs: cooked pork shoulder, lean and fat (145g cooked) = 318 kcal/34g P/19g F; brown sugar + vinegar cooking sauce = 17 kcal/4g C. Calories from macros = 335 kcal. Sodium is not counted as a macro; the original 2 tsp rub salt and garlic salt are replaced with 1 tsp total kosher salt plus garlic powder.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 8 portions)
**Ingredients:**
- 2.5 kg boneless pork shoulder / **espaldilla de cerdo**, trimmed only of hard exterior fat
- 1 Tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 2 medium onions, quartered
- 180 ml apple-cider vinegar
- 120 ml water or unsalted chicken stock
- 2 tsp GF Worcestershire sauce
- 2 tsp sugar
- 1 tsp red-pepper flakes
- 1 tsp garlic powder (not garlic salt)
- 1 tsp dry mustard
- 1/4 tsp cayenne

**Method:**
1. Heat oven to 150°C / 300°F. Mix sugar, salt, paprika, and pepper; rub over pork. Put onions in a lidded Dutch oven and set pork on top.
2. Whisk vinegar, water/stock, Worcestershire, sugar, pepper flakes, garlic powder, mustard, and cayenne. Pour around—not over—the pork. Cover tightly.
3. Braise 3 1/2–4 1/2 hours, until fork-tender and about 93°C / 200°F internally. Rest 20 minutes.
4. Reserve cooking liquid, shred, then **chop** the pork with a knife or cleaver. Toss with only enough defatted liquid to moisten. Pack extra liquid separately.

**Storage:** Fridge 3–4 days; freeze portions needed for Monday/Tuesday next week up to 3 months. Reheat covered with 1–2 Tbsp juices to 74°C / 165°F.

#### Scaling Notes
The source’s salt was reduced by half and garlic salt omitted; acid, pepper, mustard, and the two table sauces restore impact without making the meat saltier. Do not salt again until tasting the cooked pork.

### 🍗 DISH 2: Lower-Salt Oven BBQ Ribs
**Source:** https://www.mccormick.com/blogs/recipes/carolina-bbq-ribs  
**Cross-check:** https://heygrillhey.com/3-2-1-ribs-perfect-fall-off-the-bone-ribs/  
**Why this recipe:** McCormick supplies a practical covered-oven method for two racks; the 4.83/5 (201-vote) cross-check supports membrane removal, covered low-and-slow cooking, then a sauced finish. A homemade no-salt commercial-rub substitute avoids the prior salty result.
**Original servings:** 8 | **Scaled to:** 8 portions

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | 395 kcal |
| Protein | 30g (30%) |
| Carbs | 5g (5%) |
| Fat | 29g (66%) |
| Fibre | 0g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
USDA cooked pork spareribs, edible portion (125g) = 382 kcal/30g P/29g F; rub sugar = 13 kcal/3g C. Calories from macros = 395 kcal. Bone weight is excluded; portion by cooked edible meat, not “two ribs” alone.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 8 portions)
**Ingredients:**
- 2 racks baby-back or loin ribs, about 1.8–2.0 kg total / **costillar de cerdo**
- 2 Tbsp yellow mustard (labelled GF)
- 2 Tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1/2 tsp dry mustard
- 1/4 tsp cayenne
- 120 ml apple-cider vinegar + 120 ml water (for the covered pan)
- Homemade Kansas City sauce, for the final glaze

**Method:**
1. Heat oven to 150°C / 300°F. Remove the membrane from the bone side of each rack. Mix the dry ingredients; reserve 1 tsp for the final finish.
2. Rub ribs with mustard, then coat with the spice mix. Put each rack on foil in a roasting tray; add vinegar and water to the tray, without washing off the rub. Seal tightly with foil.
3. Bake 2 1/2–3 hours until tender and the meat has pulled back from the bones. Open carefully.
4. Brush lightly with KC sauce; broil 2–4 minutes until glossy, watching constantly. Rest 10 minutes, cut, and give each portion 30 ml extra sauce separately.

**Storage:** Best Wed–Sat; freeze next-week portions on cook day. Reheat covered at 150°C / 300°F with a splash of water or sauce.

#### Scaling Notes
The source uses a salted commercial rub. This batch uses only 1 tsp salt across eight servings and no salty bottled rub; the sauces stay separate, so sodium is controllable.

### 🍗 DISH 3: Oven-Baked BBQ Chicken
**Source:** https://www.recipetineats.com/oven-baked-bbq-chicken/  
**Cross-check:** https://www.tasteofhome.com/recipes/barbecued-chicken-legs/  
**Why this recipe:** RecipeTin Eats is highly tested; this recipe is rated 4.95/5 from 37 votes and braises bone-in chicken in sauce before broiling. The Taste of Home Test Kitchen method corroborates the bake-and-baste approach. It gives a leaner protein alongside pork.
**Original servings:** 5–6 | **Scaled to:** 9 portions

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | 360 kcal |
| Protein | 33g (37%) |
| Carbs | 19g (21%) |
| Fat | 17g (43%) |
| Fibre | 1g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
RecipeTin Eats’ verified card is 392 kcal/33g P/24g C/17g F per 256g serving. This version uses the same chicken quantity per serving but less added sugar/sauce (about 5g fewer carbohydrate), yielding 360 kcal by 33×4 + 19×4 + 17×9.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 9 portions)
**Ingredients:**
- 2.4 kg bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs and drumsticks / **muslos y piernas**
- 375 ml homemade Kansas City sauce (below)
- 240 ml water
- 1 Tbsp apple-cider vinegar
- 1 tsp mustard powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper

**Method:**
1. Heat oven to 200°C / 390°F. Whisk sauce, water, vinegar, mustard, onion powder, salt, and pepper in two glass or ceramic baking dishes.
2. Add chicken, turn to coat, and arrange skin-side down. Bake 20 minutes.
3. Turn chicken skin-side up, spoon sauce over it, and bake 30 minutes more. Chicken must reach at least 74°C / 165°F at the thickest point.
4. Skim excess rendered fat if desired. Broil 2–3 minutes to caramelise; do not walk away. Pack 30 ml additional sauce separately.

**Storage:** Fridge 3–4 days; freeze promptly for the following Monday/Tuesday. Reheat covered until 74°C / 165°F.

#### Scaling Notes
Sauce and salt are intentionally below linear scale: use the prepared sauce for flavour and offer more at the table rather than concentrating salt on the chicken.

### 🥬 DISH 4: Extra-Large Low-Salt Collard Greens
**Source:** https://www.seriouseats.com/collard-greens-ham-hocks-recipe  
**Cross-check:** https://www.simplyrecipes.com/recipes/southern_style_collard_greens/  
**Why this recipe:** Serious Eats’ ham-hock braise makes eight servings from 3 lb greens; Simply Recipes (5/5 from 17 ratings) confirms the same stock/ham-hock/vinegar sequence. This is a **triple batch**—enough greens for every plate, not a token side.
**Original servings:** 8 | **Scaled to:** 25 portions

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | 105 kcal |
| Protein | 8g (30%) |
| Carbs | 10g (38%) |
| Fat | 4g (34%) |
| Fibre | 6g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
USDA raw collards (170g raw equivalent) = 54 kcal/4.8g P/9.5g C/6.8g fibre; cooked smoked ham hock meat (20g edible) = 45 kcal/4g P/4g F; stock/onion = ~6 kcal. Calories from macros round to 105 kcal. Actual ham-hock yield varies; do not add salt before tasting.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 25 generous portions)
**Ingredients:**
- 4.25 kg / 9.4 lb fresh collard greens, stemmed and cut into ribbons (or **acelga** if collards are unavailable)
- 2.1 kg / 4.7 lb meaty smoked ham hocks, ideally low-sodium
- 6 medium onions, sliced
- 12 garlic cloves, crushed
- 6 L unsalted or low-sodium chicken stock
- 180 ml apple-cider vinegar, plus more at table
- 2 tsp black pepper
- GF hot sauce, to serve

**Method:**
1. Use **two large pots**. Divide hocks, onion, garlic, and stock between them. Simmer covered 2–3 hours until the hocks are tender.
2. Remove hocks; pull off meat, discard bone/skin, and return meat to the pots. Add collards in batches as they wilt.
3. Simmer uncovered about 30–45 minutes until tender. Add vinegar and pepper. Taste before adding any salt; provide hot sauce and vinegar at the table.

**Storage:** Greens improve overnight. Fridge 3–4 days; freeze in cooking liquid up to 3 months. Reheat gently with pot liquor.

#### Scaling Notes
This is 3.125× the source quantity, not the prior 2× batch. It needs two large pots; do not crowd raw greens into one pot. Salt is omitted because ham hocks and stock vary greatly in sodium.

### 🍞 DISH 5: Gluten-Free Cornbread
**Source:** https://whatgreatgrandmaate.com/gluten-free-cornbread-no-flour/  
**Cross-check:** https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/gluten-free-spoonbread-recipe  
**Why this recipe:** The flourless cornmeal recipe is 5/5 from 16 votes and produces 16 slices with familiar Southern BBQ texture; King Arthur independently validates a GF corn-based baked side. It is naturally wheat-free only with certified GF cornmeal.
**Original servings:** 16 | **Scaled to:** 16 slices (13 used, 3 frozen)

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | 160 kcal |
| Protein | 4g (10%) |
| Carbs | 25g (63%) |
| Fat | 5g (28%) |
| Fibre | 2g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
USDA cornmeal (28g) = 104 kcal/22g C/2g P; yogurt, egg, honey, and butter/ghee per slice add 56 kcal/2g P/3g C/5g F. Calories from macros = 161 kcal (rounding).

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 16 slices)
**Ingredients:**
- 2 cups / 300g certified GF medium or coarse cornmeal
- 2 tsp baking powder (GF-labelled)
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 360g plain yogurt or sour cream
- 60 ml honey
- 60 ml melted unsalted butter, ghee, or neutral oil
- 1 large egg
- 1 tsp vanilla

**Method:**
1. Heat oven to 190°C / 375°F. Grease and line an 20cm / 8-inch square pan.
2. Mix cornmeal, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Whisk yogurt, honey, butter, egg, and vanilla separately; fold wet into dry only until combined.
3. Bake 35–40 minutes until golden and a tester comes out clean. Cool 10 minutes; cut 16 pieces.

**Storage:** Fridge 3–4 days; freeze the three spare slices immediately. Microwave covered briefly or warm in oven.

#### Scaling Notes
Make one standard pan rather than a thicker doubled pan; thickness changes baking time. Cornmeal must be certified GF—not masa harina, cornstarch, or an unlabeled mill product.

### 🥔 DISH 6: Lower-Salt Creamy Mashed Potatoes
**Source:** https://www.recipetineats.com/mashed-potato/  
**Cross-check:** https://www.seriouseats.com/ultra-fluffy-mashed-potatoes-recipe  
**Why this recipe:** RecipeTin Eats (4.97/5 from 31 ratings) gives a simple, reliable make-ahead mash; Serious Eats confirms fully tender potatoes, butter first, warm dairy, and gentle mashing. This batch is portioned smaller to keep rib meals in range.
**Original servings:** 6 | **Scaled to:** 12 portions

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Calories | 210 kcal |
| Protein | 5g (10%) |
| Carbs | 34g (65%) |
| Fat | 6g (26%) |
| Fibre | 4g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
USDA potato (188g raw per portion) = 145 kcal/33g C/4g P; unsalted butter (7.5g) = 54 kcal/6g F; whole milk (20ml) = 12 kcal/1g P/1g C/1g F. Calories from macros = ~210 kcal.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 12 portions)
**Ingredients:**
- 2.25 kg potatoes, peeled and cut into 2.5cm cubes
- 90g unsalted butter, cubed
- 240 ml warm milk
- 1 tsp kosher salt for cooking water
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt to finish, then taste
- Black pepper; chives optional

**Method:**
1. Cover potatoes with cold water and 1 tsp salt. Boil, then simmer 15–20 minutes until they fall apart when pierced.
2. Drain very well; return to the dry hot pot for 1 minute. Mash by hand.
3. Fold in butter first, then warm milk, finishing salt, and pepper. Never blend or use a food processor.

**Storage:** Fridge 3–4 days or freeze up to 3 months. Reheat covered with a splash of milk.

#### Scaling Notes
The 1.5× ingredient batch is divided into 12, rather than the source’s six large servings. Salt is restrained in both water and finish; taste after dairy is incorporated.

### 🫙 DISH 7: Two Homemade Barbecue Sauces
**Carolina source:** https://www.seriouseats.com/eastern-north-carolina-barbecue-sauce  
**KC source:** https://www.seriouseats.com/sauced-kansas-city-style-barbecue-sauce-recipe  
**Why these recipes:** Serious Eats distinguishes bright Eastern Carolina vinegar-pepper sauce from thick, sweet-tangy Kansas City tomato sauce. The KC sauce works for ribs and chicken; both work with the chopped pork. Neither uses soy sauce or flour.

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Carolina (30ml) | Kansas City (30ml) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Calories | 8 kcal | 36 kcal |
| Protein | 0g | 0g |
| Carbs | 2g | 8g |
| Fat | 0g | 0g |
| Fibre | 0g | 0g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
USDA vinegar/spices make the Carolina sauce essentially negligible; the KC serving is chiefly ketchup, molasses, and brown sugar. Sauce is counted in meal averages as one 30ml serving, not unlimited free-pouring.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes about 1.1 L total)
**Ingredients — Carolina vinegar-pepper sauce (about 500 ml):**
- 360 ml apple-cider vinegar
- 120 ml white vinegar
- 1 Tbsp red-pepper flakes
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 2 tsp sugar
- 1/4 tsp salt (optional; start without)
- 2 tsp GF hot sauce

**Ingredients — Kansas City tomato sauce (about 600 ml):**
- 2 Tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 small yellow onion, finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 480 ml GF ketchup, preferably lower sodium
- 80 ml molasses
- 70g dark brown sugar
- 80 ml apple-cider vinegar
- 2 Tbsp GF yellow mustard
- 2 tsp chilli powder
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1/2 tsp cayenne

**Method:**
1. For Carolina sauce, shake all ingredients in a clean jar until sugar dissolves. Refrigerate at least overnight.
2. For KC sauce, soften onion in butter for 5 minutes; add garlic for 30 seconds. Add remaining ingredients, simmer 30 minutes until slightly thick, then blend smooth if desired. Cool quickly.
3. Label both sauces GF and date them. Pack 30ml cups; reserve KC sauce for chicken/rib glaze.

**Storage:** Refrigerate and use clean utensils. Keep 3–4 days; freeze any excess sauce. These are not shelf-stable canning recipes.

#### Scaling Notes
No added salt in the KC sauce and only optional 1/4 tsp in the Carolina batch. Ketchup, mustard, hot sauce, and commercial liquid smoke (not used here) differ substantially in sodium—taste after chilling, not while hot.

### ⚠️ NOTES & CONSIDERATIONS

- **Salt control:** Use unsalted stock, unsalted butter, no commercial BBQ rub, and labelled GF condiments. Smoked hocks can be very salty: salt collards only after tasting.
- **Greens volume:** 4.25kg raw greens is intentionally much larger. Cook in two pots; if the shop only has acelga, buy the same weight but simmer it only 15–20 minutes after the hocks are tender.
- **GF safety:** Check each bottle of ketchup, mustard, Worcestershire, stock, hot sauce, baking powder, and cornmeal. No regular soy sauce, malt vinegar, or unlabeled spice blends.
- **Meal range:** Estimated plates run about 600–690 kcal when one 30ml sauce cup is used. Ribs are the fattest protein; do not add a second cornbread slice on rib days if holding the target.
- **Cook mapping:** Wednesday: pork + collards + sauces (longest/freezer-friendly; about 5.5 hr). Thursday: ribs + mash + cornbread (about 4.5 hr). Friday: BBQ chicken (about 1.25 hr; freshest). Chop onions/garlic for all dishes Tuesday. Sunday night thaw Monday pork/chicken portions; Monday night thaw Tuesday rib/pork portions from Friday freezer stock.
- **Source and quantity checks:** Each chosen recipe was fetched/read and quantity-checked against the linked independent recipe. The main adjustments are explicitly lower salt and larger greens; they are deliberate, not transcription errors.
