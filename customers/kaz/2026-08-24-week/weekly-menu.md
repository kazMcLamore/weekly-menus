### 🍗 DISH 1: Classic Oven-Baked Shepherd's Pie
**Source:** https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/no-fuss-shepherds-pie  
**Cross-check:** https://www.noshtastic.com/easy-shepherds-pie/ (GF-specific, 5★/164 votes)  
**Why this recipe:** BBC Good Food's no-fuss shepherd's pie is rated 4.8★ from 624 ratings — exceptional validation. It uses **no flour** (the gravy thickens by reduction), so making it gluten-free needs only two swaps: GF Worcestershire sauce and GF beef bouillon. It is explicitly freezable (freeze assembled before baking, or bake then freeze portions). Filling of Kaz's choice: **lamb if available Tuesday, beef (cottage pie) as the fallback** — BBC and Noshtastic both confirm lamb = shepherd's pie, beef = cottage pie, identical method.

**Original servings:** 4 | **Scaled to:** 10 portions (2.5× BBC), 8 assigned + 2 buffer  

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Calories | ~600 kcal |
| Protein | 32g (21%) |
| Carbs | 44g (29%) |
| Fat | 34g (51%) |
| Fibre | 5g |

*Plate with green beans (~55 kcal): ~655 kcal.*

#### Macro Calculation Summary
BBC publishes 657 kcal / 39g fat / 43g carbs / 32g protein per serving. For this batch the mash butter is reduced ~40% (85g→~34g per 4-serving equivalent) and the lamb fat is drained after browning, cutting roughly 55 kcal and 5g fat per portion → ~600 kcal / 34g fat. Carbs and protein are unchanged (potato 44g carbs, lamb 32g protein). USDA cross-check: 125g cooked lamb ≈ 282 kcal/25P/21F (drained), 225g potato ≈ 173 kcal/4P/38C, ~15g butter ≈ 108 kcal/12F, oil/onion/carrot/tomato purée ≈ 70 kcal — sums to ~633 kcal, corroborating the ~600 figure. Fat is high because the dish is lamb + butter + potato; this is the richest plate of the week (balanced by the lean fish day).

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 10 portions)
**Ingredients:**
- 1.25kg / 2.75lb ground **lamb** (or ground beef — cottage pie fallback)
- 2.5 tbsp sunflower or vegetable oil
- 2–3 large onions (~400g), chopped
- 6–7 medium carrots (~450g), chopped
- 5 tbsp tomato purée (paste)
- 2.5 tbsp **GF Worcestershire sauce**
- 1.25 litres **GF beef stock** (GF bouillon + water)
- 2.25kg potatoes (floury variety preferred), peeled and quartered
- 150g butter (reduced from the BBC 212g at 2.5×)
- 7–8 tbsp milk (~110ml)
- Salt and black pepper to taste

**Method:**
1. Heat the oil in a large deep pan. Soften the onions and carrots for 5 minutes.
2. Turn up the heat, crumble in the lamb/beef and brown thoroughly. **Drain off most of the excess fat** (keep ~1 tbsp for flavour).
3. Stir in the tomato purée and GF Worcestershire; fry 2 minutes.
4. Pour in the beef stock, bring to a simmer, cover and cook **40 minutes**, uncovering halfway so the gravy reduces and thickens.
5. Meanwhile, heat the oven to **180°C / fan 160°C / gas 4**. Boil the potatoes in salted water 12–15 min until tender; drain and steam-dry, then mash with the 150g butter and milk. Season.
6. Put the mince filling into a large ovenproof dish (or 2 dishes), top with the mash, ruffle with a fork. *(Can be cooled, chilled and frozen at this point.)*
7. Bake **20–25 min** until the top is coloured and the filling bubbles at the edges. Rest 5 min before portioning.

**Storage:** Fridge 4 days. Freezer 1 month (freeze assembled-before-baking, then bake from frozen at 160°C/fan 140°C for 1 hr–1 hr 20 min; or bake first then freeze individual portions and reheat). Reheat microwave or oven.

#### Scaling Notes
- Scaled 2.5× from a 4-serving recipe → 10 servings (clean).
- **Butter reduced ~40%** per Kaz's "lighter hand with rich dishes" feedback; this lowers fat and calories without hurting the mash.
- **Salt:** BBC adds none beyond the stock; with GF bouillon (which is salty) taste the filling before adding any extra salt.
- No flour in the source, so no thickener swap is needed — the 40-minute reduction does the work. If the cook wants a thicker gravy, use 1 tbsp cornstarch slurry (GF) rather than flour.

### 🍗 DISH 2: Butter Chicken (Murgh Makhani) — repeat recipe
**Source:** https://www.recipetineats.com/butter-chicken/  
**Why this recipe:** This is the **same previously approved RecipeTin Eats recipe used 2026-06-15** (4.96★ from ~1,100 votes, adapted from chef Luke Mangan). Per Kaz's recipe-continuity rule it is reused exactly — only rescaled. Naturally gluten-free.

**Original servings:** 3–4 | **Scaled to:** 9 portions (3×), 8 assigned + 1 buffer  

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Calories | 402 kcal |
| Protein | 40g (40%) |
| Carbs | 10g (10%) |
| Fat | 23g (52%) |
| Fibre | 1g |

*RecipeTin official nutrition per 310g serving (chicken + sauce, no rice). Plate with arroz rojo + dal → ~690 kcal.*

#### Macro Calculation Summary
RecipeTin's published nutrition is 402 kcal / 39.8g protein / 9.8g carbs / 23.4g fat per serving (no rice). The richness comes from 1 cup heavy cream + 2 tbsp ghee per batch; this matches the 2026-06-15 plan's estimate (~435 kcal no rice / 635 with rice) — confirmed, not changed. USDA sanity check: 250g skinless chicken thigh (cooked) ≈ 400 kcal/43P/30F (some fat renders), 80g cream ≈ 270 kcal/29F, 9g ghee ≈ 80 kcal, passata/yogurt/sugar ≈ 55 kcal → ~805 raw; after render/drain the effective portion lands ~400–450 kcal, consistent with RecipeTin's card.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 9 portions, 3× original)
**Ingredients:**
- 2.25kg boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite-size pieces
- 1.5 cups plain full-fat yogurt
- 3 tbsp lemon juice
- 3 tsp turmeric powder
- 6 tsp garam masala
- 1.5 tsp chilli powder (pure, or cayenne)
- 3 tsp ground cumin
- 3 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 6 cloves garlic, crushed
- 6 tbsp ghee (or butter)
- 3 cups tomato passata (pureed tomatoes)
- 3 cups heavy cream
- 3 tbsp sugar
- 3.5 tsp salt (reduced ~7% from 3.75 tsp)
- Fresh coriander/cilantro for garnish

**Method:**
1. Combine yogurt, lemon juice, turmeric, garam masala, chilli powder, cumin, ginger and garlic. Add chicken, coat well. **Marinate overnight (Tuesday night)** — minimum 3 hours.
2. Heat ghee in a large skillet over high heat. Remove chicken from marinade (don't shake off excess) and cook ~3 minutes until white all over. Work in batches — don't crowd the pan.
3. Add passata, cream, sugar, salt and any remaining marinade from the bowl. Stir well.
4. Reduce heat to low and simmer **20 minutes** until the sauce thickens and deepens in colour.
5. Garnish with coriander. (Rice packed alongside.)

**Storage:** Fridge 4–5 days. Freezes well up to 3 months (sauce may separate slightly on thaw — stir while reheating). Reheat gently stovetop or microwave.

#### Scaling Notes
- Scaled 3× from a 3–4 serving recipe → 9 portions.
- **Spices scaled linearly** (3×) — acceptable here because the spices disperse through a large sauce volume (3 cups passata + 3 cups cream); the dish is saucy, not a dry rub.
- **Salt reduced ~7%** (3.75 tsp → 3.5 tsp) per Kaz's preference for a lighter salt hand; taste before adding more.
- Recipe and ratios are unchanged from 2026-06-15 (continuity).

### 🍗 DISH 3: Pescado a la Veracruzana (Veracruz-Style Fish)
**Source:** https://mexicanfoodjournal.com/veracruz-style-white-fish/  
**Cross-check:** https://norecipes.com/fish-veracruz-recipe-pescado-veracruzana/ (Marc Matsumoto — resolves the onion quantity)  
**Why this recipe:** Mexican Food Journal's Douglas Cullen lived in Mexico 25 years; the recipe is authentic, fully stovetop, and built around local fish (huachinango / red snapper — readily available in Playa). It is naturally gluten-free. The source's ingredient list omits the onion amount (flagged repeatedly in the comments); the NoRecipes cross-check fixes this at ~1 medium onion per 4 servings. The source's own nutrition (213 kcal) undercounts the oil; the macros below are recomputed from USDA.

**Original servings:** 4 | **Scaled to:** 9 portions (2.25×), 8 assigned + 1 buffer  

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Calories | ~330 kcal |
| Protein | 41g (50%) |
| Carbs | 5g (6%) |
| Fat | 16g (44%) |
| Fibre | 2g |

*Plate with arroz rojo + dal → ~620 kcal.*

#### Macro Calculation Summary
Per portion (~150g fish + sauce): red snapper 150g ≈ 150 kcal/30P/2F (USDA); sauce — 3 tbsp oil/4 portions ≈ 100 kcal, 1 medium tomato + ¼ onion + garlic/olives/capers/broth ≈ 80 kcal, 7 olives ≈ 30 kcal → ~180 kcal sauce, 1P, 4C, 13F. Total ≈ 330 kcal, 41P, 5C, 16F. (Source said 213 kcal — it omits most of the oil; the figure above is the honest USDA count with the reduced oil.)

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 9 portions, 2.25×)
**Ingredients:**
- 9 white-fish fillets (~150g each, ~1.35kg total) — huachinango (red snapper) preferred; tilapia/mojarra or any firm white fish works
- 11 medium tomatoes, roughly chopped
- 2–3 medium white onions (~300g), chopped
- 9 cloves garlic, chopped
- 2.25 tbsp dried thyme
- 2 tsp black pepper
- 2 tsp dried oregano (substitute for the source's epazote — author-recommended, easier to find)
- 2 tsp salt + more to taste
- ~12 bay leaves
- ~1 cup green olives, pitted (≈63 olives)
- ~½ cup capers (~3 tbsp)
- 2.25 cups low-sodium chicken broth (GF)
- 3 tbsp cooking oil (for the sauce)
- 1 tbsp cooking oil (to sear the fish)

**Method:**
1. In a large deep pan, heat 3 tbsp oil over medium. Fry the onion and garlic 4 minutes.
2. Add the tomatoes, black pepper and salt. Reduce heat to low and cook 15 minutes until the tomatoes release their liquid. Crush the tomatoes/onion/garlic with a masher.
3. Stir in thyme, oregano and bay leaves. Add the broth, olives and capers. Simmer 15 minutes more.
4. In a separate pan, heat 1 tbsp oil over medium and lightly brown the fish fillets on both sides (don't over-brown).
5. Nestle the fillets into the simmering sauce and cook **10 minutes** until the fish is cooked through and the sauce thickens slightly. Adjust salt. Spoon sauce over when portioning.

**Storage:** Fridge 3 days (fish is best fresh — eat Fri/Sat first). Freezes acceptably for Mon–Tue (sauce protects the fish); reheat gently so the fish doesn't overcook. This is the weakest freezer of the week.

#### Scaling Notes
- Scaled 2.25× from a 4-serving recipe → 9 portions.
- **Oil reduced from the source's 6 tbsp (3 for sauce + 3 for fish) to 4 tbsp (3 + 1).** The fish is simmered in sauce anyway, so 3 tbsp for searing is excessive; 1 tbsp is enough and saves ~180 kcal per batch.
- **Onion quantity taken from the NoRecipes cross-check** (~1 medium per 4 servings) because the source omits it.
- **Oregano (½ tsp per 4 servings) replaces epazote** — the author himself recommends this in the comments, and oregano is easier to find in Playa.
- Bay leaves: source uses 6 per 4 servings (a lot); scaled to ~12 for 9 portions — remove before portioning.

### 🥬 DISH 4: Dal Tadka (Tempered Yellow Lentils)
**Source:** https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/dal-tadka/  
**Cross-check:** https://www.vegrecipesofindia.com/restaurant-style-dal-tadka/ (Dassana — corroborates toor dal + cumin/garlic/red-chili tadka)  
**Why this recipe:** Swasthi's Recipes is one of the most trusted Indian home-cooking sites; this dal tadka is 5★ from 219 votes. It is naturally gluten-free (with one watch-point: asafoetida/hing is often cut with wheat — **skip it or use a certified-GF hing**). Stovetop (no Instant Pot assumed), and it reheats beautifully — Sinfully Spicy and Swasthi both note it tastes better the next day, ideal for a Wed cook eaten through the week.

**Original servings:** 4 | **Scaled to:** ~20 half-side portions (2.5×), 18 assigned + 2 buffer  

#### Per-Portion (half-side) Macro Breakdown
| Nutrient | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Calories | ~175 kcal |
| Protein | 8g (18%) |
| Carbs | 21g (48%) |
| Fat | 7g (36%) |
| Fibre | ~10g |

*Served as a side (~½ cup / 130g) alongside butter chicken or fish + rice.*

#### Macro Calculation Summary
Swasthi publishes 351 kcal / 16g protein / 43g carbs / 14g fat / 19g fibre per full serving (1 cup cooked, 4 servings from 1 cup dry dal). A half-side (~½ cup) is ~175 kcal, 8P, 21C, 7F, ~10g fibre. USDA check: 50g dry toor dal ≈ 170 kcal/11P/30C/1F; with ~8g oil + ~7g ghee tadka + tomato/onion ≈ 130 kcal/12F → ~300 kcal per full serving, ~150 per half-side; consistent with Swasthi's 351 (she counts a little more ghee). High fibre (toor dal) — a positive for this week.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes ~20 half-side portions, 2.5×)
**Ingredients:**
- 2.5 cups (500g) toor dal (split pigeon peas) — *sub: yellow split peas or masoor (red lentils) if toor dal is unavailable; see supplement*
- 7.5 cups water (to cook dal)
- ~4 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds (reduced from 1.25 tsp)
- 2.5 tsp each garlic and ginger, finely chopped
- 2–3 medium onions, finely chopped
- 2 green chilies, chopped (medium heat — use 1 for mild)
- 5 medium tomatoes (~2.5 cups chopped)
- 0.5 tsp turmeric (reduced from 0.625 tsp)
- 1.25 tsp red chilli powder (reduced from 1.9 tsp)
- 1 tsp garam masala (reduced from 1.25 tsp)
- 2 tsp salt (reduced ~20% from 2.5 tsp)
- 2.5 tbsp kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves), crushed
- 2–3 tbsp coriander leaves, chopped
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges (to serve)

**Tadka (tempering):**
- 4 tbsp ghee (reduced from 5 tbsp)
- 1.25 tsp cumin seeds
- 4 dried red chilies
- 2 tbsp garlic, finely chopped
- ⅛ tsp **GF-certified asafoetida (hing)** — or skip entirely (common hing has wheat)
- 0.6 tsp red chilli powder

**Method:**
1. Rinse the dal thoroughly (4–5 changes of water). Put in a pot with 7.5 cups water, bring to a boil, then simmer covered 25–30 minutes until soft and mushy (skim any scum). Lightly mash, keeping some texture. (Pressure cooker: 3–4 whistles.)
2. In a pan, heat 4 tbsp oil, add 1 tsp cumin and let it splutter. Add ginger-garlic, fry lightly. Add onions + green chili and fry until golden.
3. Reduce heat; add turmeric, red chilli powder and garam masala; stir. Add tomatoes + salt; sauté until soft.
4. Stir the masala into the cooked dal; simmer 6–7 minutes. Adjust salt and consistency (it thickens on cooling). Stir in kasuri methi and coriander; turn off heat.
5. **Tadka:** in a small pan, heat 4 tbsp ghee, add 1.25 tsp cumin (and mustard seeds if liked), then dried red chilies, then garlic — fry lightly, do not brown. Stir in hing and 0.6 tsp red chilli powder; immediately pour the hot tadka over the dal.
6. Serve with a lemon wedge.

**Storage:** Fridge 4–5 days (better on day 2–3). Freezes well up to 2 months. Reheat stovetop or microwave; add water to loosen.

#### Scaling Notes
- Scaled 2.5× on lentils and water (linear — absorption).
- **Spices scaled sub-linearly (~0.7–0.8× of linear 2.5×)** per batch-scaling guidance: turmeric, red chilli, garam masala and tadka cumin all reduced. Lentil dishes carry spices in a large water volume so the reduction is conservative.
- **Salt reduced ~20%** (2.5 tsp → 2 tsp) per Kaz's lighter-salt preference.
- **Tadka ghee reduced** from 5 tbsp to 4 tbsp to trim fat.
- **Hing skipped unless GF-certified** — common hing is cut with wheat flour (GF risk). The dal is excellent without it.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes 10 side portions, 3.5×)
**Ingredients:**
- 875g green beans, trimmed
- 1 tbsp salt (for the blanching water)
- 3.5 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 7 garlic cloves, finely minced (~7 tsp) — **use a knife, not a garlic press** (press makes it wet/pasty and it burns)
- ~0.5 tsp salt + black pepper

**Method:**

3. Season with salt and pepper. Portion.

**Storage:** Fridge 4 days (best fresh; garlic crispiness softens on storage). Freezes acceptably (blanch stops enzyme action); texture fine, garlic flavour holds. Reheat microwave or quick skillet.

#### Scaling Notes
- Scaled 3.5× from a 2–4 serving recipe → 10 small side portions (~87g each).
- Blanch in two batches if the pot isn't large enough to keep the water boiling.
- Sauté in two batches so the beans sear rather than steam.

### 🍚 ARROZ ROJO (Mexican Red Rice) — repeat recipe, carb
**Source:** https://www.rickbayless.com/recipe/red-tomato-rice/  
**Cross-check:** https://www.mexicoinmykitchen.com/mexican-rice-recipe-authentic/ (Mely Martínez, 4.75★/39 votes)  
**Why this recipe:** The same Rick Bayless arroz rojo approved 2026-06-29 — rice toasted in oil, then cooked in a blended tomato/onion/garlic puree with chicken broth. Reused exactly per continuity, only rescaled. Naturally GF (use GF bouillon).

**Original yield:** ~6 cups cooked (6–8 servings) | **Scaled to:** ~7 cups, ~22 portions (2×), 16 assigned + 6 buffer  

#### Per-Portion Macro Breakdown (1/3 cup / 80g cooked)
| Nutrient | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Calories | 115 kcal |
| Protein | 2g |
| Carbs | 22g |
| Fat | 1g |

#### Macro Calculation Summary
Long-grain white rice cooked (80g) ≈ 104 kcal / 2g P / 22g C / 0.2g F (USDA). Tomato puree contributes ~3 kcal; oil 3 tbsp per 6 cups → ~1g per 1/3 cup ≈ 9 kcal. Total ≈ 115 kcal. Consistent with the 2026-06-29 figure.

#### Scaled Batch Recipe (Makes ~7 cups, 2×)
**Ingredients:**
- 2 cans (15 oz / 425g each) diced tomatoes (fire-roasted preferred), lightly drained
- 1 medium white onion, roughly chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, peeled and halved
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 3 cups white rice (medium-grain preferred, long-grain fine)
- 3.5 cups chicken broth (GF bouillon + water)
- 6 serrano chiles (or 4 jalapeños), slit lengthwise
- ½ cup coarsely chopped fresh parsley
- 1.5 tsp salt (if using salted broth; 3 tsp if unsalted/water)

**Method:**
1. Blend the drained tomatoes, onion and garlic to a smooth puree.
2. In a wide pot (≈8 inch / 3 quart, tight lid), heat the oil over medium. Add the rice and stir frequently until the grains turn from translucent to milky white, 5–6 minutes (some light browning is fine).
3. Add the tomato puree and stir; cook 2–3 minutes until it reduces and looks somewhat dry.
4. Add the broth, bring to a full boil, then add the slit chiles, parsley and salt. Stir, scraping down any rice on the sides.
5. Cover and cook on the **lowest heat for 20 minutes. Do NOT lift the lid or stir.**
6. Remove from heat; fluff gently with a fork, re-cover and rest 5 minutes. Remove chiles (or leave as decoration). Yields ~7 cups.

**Storage:** Fridge 4–5 days. Reheat microwave with a sprinkle of water. **Do not long-freeze** — texture degrades. (Short Mon–Tue freeze acceptable this week; see freezer caveat above.)

- **Salt** scales with the liquid volume (it dissolves in the broth), so 2× is fine; 1.5 tsp with salted broth.
- Extra yield (~6 portions) is intentional buffer.

### ⚠️ NOTES & CONSIDERATIONS

- **Macro profile is fat-forward this week (~43% fat vs the notional 30%).** The drivers are the lamb pie (lamb + butter) and the butter chicken (heavy cream + ghee). Calories are on target (~655/meal) and protein is strong (~45g), but if Kaz wants to move toward 30/40/30: (a) drain the lamb fat aggressively and cut the mash butter further; (b) scoop a larger portion of arroz rojo (½ cup) on the butter-chicken and fish plates to lift carbs; (c) reduce the dal tadka ghee to 3 tbsp. Kaz's 30/40/30 split is marked "assumed, to be confirmed" in his profile, so this is presented as-is with levers noted.
- **Shepherd's pie = the richest plate (~655 kcal with beans), fish = the leanest (~620 kcal).** The week balances out across days.
- **Lamb availability:** cordero is stocked at Chedraui/Walmart but pricier and not always reliable. If unavailable Tuesday, use beef mince (cottage pie) — identical method, BBC and Noshtastic both endorse it.
- **GF watch-points this week:** (1) Worcestershire — standard Lea & Perrins contains barley malt; use a GF Worcestershire. (2) Bouillon — check the beef and chicken bouillon labels for gluten. (3) Asafoetida (hing) in the dal — common hing is cut with wheat; skip it or use a GF-certified brand. (4) The pie uses no flour, so no thickener swap is needed. Everything else (rice, dal, butter chicken, Veracruzana, green beans) is naturally GF.
- **Fish is the weakest freezer** — 4 portions are frozen for Mon–Tue. Eat the Friday/Saturday fish fresh; if Kaz prefers, swap a Mon–Tue fish plate for an extra pie or butter-chicken plate and eat that fish fresh Saturday.
- **Arroz rojo freeze caveat** — best fresh; the Mon–Tue rice is a short 2–4 day freeze and will be fine. Fallback: cook plain white rice Monday if the thawed arroz rojo is too soft.
- **Eat-by order:** Veracruzana fish first (Fri–Sat fresh; weakest freezer). Shepherd's pie and butter chicken hold and freeze well — fine for Mon–Tue. Dal improves on day 2–3.
- **Toor dal sourcing:** DAC Market (30 Av. entre Calle 14 y 14 Bis) or Amazon Mexico; if unavailable, yellow split peas or masoor (red lentils) work (red lentils cook faster, ~20 min). Full substitution detail in the weekly supplement.
- **Continuity:** Butter chicken and arroz rojo reuse the exact previously approved recipes (2026-06-15 and 2026-06-29 respectively), rescaled only. No silent substitutions.
